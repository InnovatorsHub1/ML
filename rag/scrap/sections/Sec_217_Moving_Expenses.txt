Sec. 217. Moving Expenses

I.R.C. § 217(a) Deduction Allowed — 
               
               
There  shall be allowed as a deduction moving expenses paid or incurred during  the
                  taxable year in connection with the commencement of work by the  taxpayer as an employee
                  or as a self-employed individual at a new  principal place of work.
I.R.C. § 217(b) Definition Of Moving Expenses


I.R.C. § 217(b)(1) In General — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this section, the term “moving expenses” means only the  reasonable
                     expenses— 

I.R.C. § 217(b)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
of  moving household goods and personal effects from the former residence to  the
                        new residence, and 


I.R.C. § 217(b)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
of  traveling (including lodging) from the former residence to the new place  of residence.
                        Such term shall not include any expenses for meals. 



I.R.C. § 217(b)(2) Individuals Other Than Taxpayer — 
                  
                  
In  the case of any individual other than the taxpayer, expenses referred to  in paragraph
                     (1) shall be taken into account only if such individual has  both the former residence
                     and the new residence as his principal place of  abode and is a member of the taxpayer's
                     household.
I.R.C. § 217(c) Conditions For Allowance — 
               
               
No  deduction shall be allowed under this section unless— 

I.R.C. § 217(c)(1)  — 
                  
                  
the  taxpayer's new principal place of work— 

I.R.C. § 217(c)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
is at  least 50 miles  farther from his former residence than was his former  principal
                        place of work, or 


I.R.C. § 217(c)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
if he  had no former principal place of work, is at least 50 miles from his  former
                        residence, and 



I.R.C. § 217(c)(2)  — 
                  
                  
either— 

I.R.C. § 217(c)(2)(A)  — 
                     
                     
during  the 12-month period immediately following his arrival in the general  location
                        of his new principal place of work, the taxpayer is a full-time  employee, in such
                        general location, during at least 39 weeks, or 


I.R.C. § 217(c)(2)(B)  — 
                     
                     
during  the 24-month period immediately following his arrival in the general  location
                        of his new principal place of work, the taxpayer is a full-time  employee or performs
                        services as a self-employed individual on a full-time  basis, in such general location,
                        during at least 78 weeks, of which not  less than 39 weeks are during the 12-month
                        period referred to in  subparagraph (A). 


For purposes of paragraph (1), the distance between two  points shall be the shortest
                        of the more commonly traveled routes between  such two points.
I.R.C. § 217(d) Rules For Application Of Subsection (c)(2)


I.R.C. § 217(d)(1)  — 
                  
                  
The  condition of subsection (c)(2)
                     shall not apply if the taxpayer is unable  to satisfy such condition by reason of— 

I.R.C. § 217(d)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
death  or disability, or 


I.R.C. § 217(d)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
involuntary  separation (other than for willful misconduct) from the service of, or
                        transfer for the benefit of, an employer after obtaining full-time  employment in
                        which the taxpayer could reasonably have been expected to  satisfy such condition. 



I.R.C. § 217(d)(2)  — 
                  
                  
If a  taxpayer has not satisfied the condition of subsection (c)(2) before the  time
                     prescribed by law
                     (including extensions thereof) for filing the  return for the taxable year during
                     which he paid or incurred moving  expenses which would otherwise be deductible under
                     this section, but may  still satisfy such condition, then such expenses may (at the
                     election of  the taxpayer)
                     be deducted for such taxable year notwithstanding subsection  (c)(2). 


I.R.C. § 217(d)(3)  — 
                  
                  
If— 

I.R.C. § 217(d)(3)(A)  — 
                     
                     
for  any taxable year moving expenses have been deducted in accordance with the  rule
                        provided in paragraph
                        (2), and 


I.R.C. § 217(d)(3)(B)  — 
                     
                     
the  condition of subsection (c)(2)
                        cannot be satisfied at the close of a  subsequent taxable year, then an amount equal
                        to the expenses which were  so deducted shall be included in gross income for the
                        first such  subsequent taxable year.
I.R.C. § 217(e)  —

I.R.C. § 217(f) Self-Employed Individual — 
               
               
For  purposes of this section, the term “self-employed individual” means an  individual
                  who performs personal services— 

I.R.C. § 217(f)(1)  — 
                  
                  
as  the owner of the entire interest in an unincorporated trade or business,  or 


I.R.C. § 217(f)(2)  — 
                  
                  
as a  partner in a partnership carrying on a trade or  business.
I.R.C. § 217(g) Rules For Members Of The Armed Forces Of The United States — 
               
               
In  the case of a member of the Armed Forces of the United States on active  duty
                  who moves pursuant to a military order and incident to a permanent  change of station— 

I.R.C. § 217(g)(1)  — 
                  
                  
the  limitations under subsection (c)
                     shall not apply; 


I.R.C. § 217(g)(2)  — 
                  
                  
any  moving and storage expenses which are furnished in kind (or for which  reimbursement
                     or an allowance is provided, but only to the extent of the  expenses paid or incurred)
                     to such member, his spouse, or his dependents,  shall not be includible in gross income,
                     and no reporting with respect to  such expenses shall be required by the Secretary
                     of Defense or the  Secretary of Transportation, as the case may be; and 


I.R.C. § 217(g)(3)  — 
                  
                  
if  moving and storage expenses are furnished in kind (or if reimbursement or  an
                     allowance for such expenses is provided) to such member's spouse and  his dependents
                     with regard to moving to a location other than the one to  which such member moves
                     (or from a location other than the one from which  such member moves), this section
                     shall apply with respect to the moving  expenses of his spouse and dependents— 

I.R.C. § 217(g)(3)(A)  — 
                     
                     
as if  his spouse commenced work as an employee at a new principal place of work 
                        at such location; and 


I.R.C. § 217(g)(3)(B)  — 
                     
                     
without  regard to the limitations under subsection (c).
I.R.C. § 217(h) Special Rules For Foreign Moves


I.R.C. § 217(h)(1) Allowance Of Certain Storage Fees — 
                  
                  
In  the case of a foreign move, for purposes of this section, the moving  expenses
                     described in subsection (b)(1)(A) include the reasonable  expenses-- 

I.R.C. § 217(h)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
of  moving household goods and personal effects to and from storage, and 


I.R.C. § 217(h)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
of  storing such goods and effects for part or all of the period during which  the
                        new place of work continues to be the taxpayer's principal place of  work. 



I.R.C. § 217(h)(2) Foreign Move — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this subsection, the term “foreign move” means the  commencement
                     of work by the taxpayer at a new principal place of work  located outside the United
                     States. 


I.R.C. § 217(h)(3) United States Defined — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this subsection and subsection (i), the term “United States”  includes
                     the possessions of the United States.
I.R.C. § 217(i) Allowance Of Deductions In Case Of Retirees Or Decedents Who Were Working Abroad


I.R.C. § 217(i)(1) In General — 
                  
                  
In  the case of any qualified retiree moving expenses or qualified survivor  moving
                     expenses— 

I.R.C. § 217(i)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
this  section (other than subsection
                        (h)) shall be applied with respect to such  expenses as if they were incurred in connection
                        with the commencement of  work by the taxpayer as an employee at a new principal place
                        of work  located within the United States, and 


I.R.C. § 217(i)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
the  limitations of subsection (c)(2)
                        shall not apply. 



I.R.C. § 217(i)(2) Qualified Retiree Moving Expenses — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of paragraph (1), the term “qualified retiree moving expenses”  means
                     any moving expenses— 

I.R.C. § 217(i)(2)(A)  — 
                     
                     
which  are incurred by an individual whose former principal place of work and  former
                        residence were outside the United States, and 


I.R.C. § 217(i)(2)(B)  — 
                     
                     
which  are incurred for a move to a new residence in the United States in  connection
                        with the bona fide retirement of the individual. 



I.R.C. § 217(i)(3) Qualified Survivor Moving Expenses — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of paragraph (1), the term “qualified survivor moving expenses”  means
                     moving expenses— 

I.R.C. § 217(i)(3)(A)  — 
                     
                     
which  are paid or incurred by the spouse or any dependent of any decedent who  (as
                        of the time of his death)
                        had a principal place of work outside the  United States, and 


I.R.C. § 217(i)(3)(B)  — 
                     
                     
which  are incurred for a move which begins within 6 months after the death of  such
                        decedent and which is to a residence in the United States from a  former residence
                        outside the United States which (as of the time of the  decedent's death)
                        was the residence of such decedent and the individual  paying or incurring the expense.
I.R.C. § 217(j) Regulations — 
               
               
The  Secretary shall prescribe such regulations as may be necessary to carry  out
                  the purposes of this section.
I.R.C. § 217(k) Suspension Of Deduction For Taxable Years 2018 Through 2025 — 
               
               
Except in the case of an individual to whom subsection (g) applies, this section shall
                  not apply to any taxable year beginning after December 31, 2017, and before January
                  1, 2026.
