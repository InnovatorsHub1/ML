Sec. 2503. Taxable Gifts

I.R.C. § 2503(a) General Definition — 
               
               
The  term “taxable gifts” means the total amount of gifts made during the  calendar
                  year, less the deductions provided in subchapter C (section 2522 
                  and following).
I.R.C. § 2503(b) Exclusions From Gifts


I.R.C. § 2503(b)(1) In General — 
                  
                  
In the case of gifts (other than gifts of future  interests in property) made to any
                     person by the donor during the calendar 
                     year, the first $10,000 of such gifts to such person shall not, for
                     purposes of subsection (a), be included in the total amount of gifts made  during
                     such year. Where there has been a transfer to any person of a  present interest in
                     property, the possibility that such interest may be  diminished by the exercise of
                     a power shall be disregarded in applying  this subsection, if no part of such interest
                     will at any time pass to any  other person. 


I.R.C. § 2503(b)(2)  Inflation Adjustment — 
                  
                  
In  the case of gifts made in a calendar year after 1998, the $10,000 amount  contained
                     in paragraph (1) shall be increased by an amount equal to-- 

I.R.C. § 2503(b)(2)(A)  — 
                     
                     
$10,000, multiplied by 


I.R.C. § 2503(b)(2)(B)  — 
                     
                     
the cost-of-living adjustment determined under section  1(f)(3) for such calendar year by substituting “calendar year 1997” for “calendar year 2016”
                        in subparagraph (A)(ii) thereof. 


If any amount as adjusted under the preceding sentence
                        is not a multiple of $1,000, such amount shall be rounded to the next  lowest multiple
                        of $1,000.
I.R.C. § 2503(c) Transfer For The Benefit Of Minor — 
               
               
No  part of a gift to an individual who has not attained the age of 21 years  on the
                  date of such transfer shall be considered a gift of a future  interest in property
                  for purposes of subsection
                  (b) if the property and  the income therefrom— 

I.R.C. § 2503(c)(1)  — 
                  
                  
may be expended by, or for the benefit of, the donee  before his attaining the age
                     of 21 years, and 


I.R.C. § 2503(c)(2)  — 
                  
                  
will to the extent not so expended— 

I.R.C. § 2503(c)(2)(A)  — 
                     
                     
pass to the donee on his attaining the age of 21 years,  and 


I.R.C. § 2503(c)(2)(B)  — 
                     
                     
in the event the donee dies before attaining the age of  21 years, be payable to the
                        estate of the donee or as he may appoint under  a general power of appointment as
                        defined in section 2514(c).

I.R.C. § 2503(e) Exclusion For Certain Transfers For Educational Expenses Or Medical Expenses


I.R.C. § 2503(e)(1) In General — 
                  
                  
Any  qualified transfer shall not be treated as a transfer of property by gift  for
                     purposes of this chapter. 


I.R.C. § 2503(e)(2) Qualified Transfer — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this subsection, the term “qualified transfer” means any  amount
                     paid on behalf of an individual— 

I.R.C. § 2503(e)(2)(A)  — 
                     
                     
as tuition to an educational organization described in  section 170(b)(1)(A)(ii) for the education or training of such individual,  or 


I.R.C. § 2503(e)(2)(B)  — 
                     
                     
to any person who provides medical care (as defined in  section 213(d))
                        with respect to such individual as payment for such  medical care.
I.R.C. § 2503(f) Waiver Of Certain Pension Rights — 
               
               
If  any individual waives, before the death of a participant, any survivor  benefit,
                  or right to such benefit, under section 401(a)(11) or 417, such  waiver shall not be treated as a transfer of property by gift for purposes
                  of this chapter.
I.R.C. § 2503(g) Treatment Of Certain Loans Of Artworks 


I.R.C. § 2503(g)(1) In General — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this subtitle, any loan of a qualified work of art shall not  be
                     treated as a transfer (and the value of such qualified work of art  shall be determined
                     as if such loan had not been made) if— 

I.R.C. § 2503(g)(1)(A)  — 
                     
                     
such loan is to an organization described in section  501(c)(3) and exempt from tax under section 501(c) (other than a private  foundation), and 


I.R.C. § 2503(g)(1)(B)  — 
                     
                     
the use of such work by such organization is related to  the purpose or function constituting
                        the basis for its exemption under  section 501. 



I.R.C. § 2503(g)(2) Definitions — 
                  
                  
For  purposes of this section— 

I.R.C. § 2503(g)(2)(A) Qualified Work Of Art — 
                     
                     
The  term “qualified work of art” means any archaeological, historic, or  creative
                        tangible personal property. 


I.R.C. § 2503(g)(2)(B) Private Foundation — 
                     
                     
The  term “private foundation” has the meaning given such term by section 509,
                        except that such term shall not include any private operating foundation
                        (as defined in section 4942(j)(3)).
